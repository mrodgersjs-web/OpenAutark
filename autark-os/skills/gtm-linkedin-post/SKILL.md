# LinkedIn post

I read this when a post is requested or a draft is handed to me to fix.

## Brief first

One idea, one audience, the evidence, the emotion it should leave, the action at the end. No
brief, no draft.

## Structure (120-220 words)

1. **Hook** — first line carries the whole post: a number, a tension, or a claim someone would
   argue with. No throat-clearing, no "I've been thinking about".
2. **Claim** — what is true, in one sentence a stranger can repeat.
3. **Evidence** — the receipt: what happened, the number, the before/after, the source.
4. **Cost** — what it took, what went wrong, what it cost to learn. This is the credibility.
5. **Next** — what you would do, concretely, in one or two lines.
6. **Question** — one question that the right reader will want to answer.

## Voice

Concrete nouns, real numbers, short sentences. The person's own phrases. Banned: unlock, empower,
leverage, synergy, disrupt, hustle, optimize, "game-changer", "excited to share", "here's the
thing", "let that sink in". No emoji unless the person uses them.

## Steps

1. Write the brief in five lines.
2. Draft the hook three ways; keep the one a competitor would not want to see.
3. Draft the body against the structure. Cut every sentence that only sounds good.
4. Read it aloud; mark the line that could be misread or needs a receipt.
5. Send to `gtm-council-review` if it carries a public claim, then to the approval queue.

## Output

`gtm/drafts/<date>-<slug>.md`: the brief, the post, the three hooks, the risk line.

## Guardrails

- Never publish, schedule or comment from here.
- No invented result, no borrowed story, no client name without permission.
