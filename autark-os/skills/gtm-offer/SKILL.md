# Offer and price

I read this when a new offer is designed, a price is set or changed, or a proposal is written.

## The offer, five parts

1. **Outcome** — the state the buyer is in afterwards, measurable, in their words.
2. **Value equation** — (dream outcome x likelihood of it) / (time to it x effort to get it).
   Put real numbers or ranges on each term, with the source. No number, no claim.
3. **Proof** — the smallest credible demonstration: a pilot, a teardown, a measured before/after.
4. **Risk reversal** — what we will do if it does not land, and under which conditions. Only
   promise what we can honour without a lawyer in the room.
5. **Price bands** — three: the smallest useful engagement, the standard, and the one where we
   are the operator not the adviser. Each band names what it includes and what it excludes.

## Steps

1. Write the outcome as the buyer would say it to their boss.
2. Fill the value equation with ranges and name the assumption behind each.
3. Set the price against the buyer's economics, not our effort: what the problem costs them per
   month, what share of that we can remove, and what we keep.
4. Write the refusal: the work this offer excludes, so scope cannot drift in later.
5. Test the sentence "this costs X and does Y" out loud. If it needs a second sentence to sound
   reasonable, the offer is wrong or the price is.

## Output

`gtm/offer-ladder.md` — three bands, each with outcome, inclusions, exclusions, price, proof.

## Guardrails

- Never invent a customer result to make the offer feel proven.
- Never promise a discount or a guarantee the person has not approved (G3/G4).
