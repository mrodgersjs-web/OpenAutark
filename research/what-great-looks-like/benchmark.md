## The evidence

### Autark — the strongest copy in the set, the weakest product presence above the fold

![Autark hero](shots/openautark.jpg)

"The most capable models ever built sit behind an empty text box. They answer when asked, forget
when you close the chat, and leave the real work — the routing, the follow-ups, the copy and paste
— to you." That is a category argument in three sentences, and it is the sharpest positioning of
any page captured. The three trust chips under the button (no account, no subscription from us,
your data lives on your PC) do more selling than most of the competitors' entire feature sections.

![Autark, the problem section](shots/openautark-mid.jpg)

What it does not do in the first screen: show the thing. Manus puts a working input box in front of
you; Lindy shows a face and a promise with references. Autark shows a wordmark and a promise. The
product appears lower, as a mock window — and the mock is a clock, not a chat doing work.

![Autark, the comparison](shots/openautark-features.jpg)

The app itself, from the repo's own documentation:

![Autark main window](shots/autark-app-main-window.png)

### Manus — the product as the hero

![Manus](shots/manus.jpg)

No headline about the company, one question, one box, and four chips that answer "what would I
even use it for" (slides, website, games, video). A discount banner sits above it, which trades
premium for pressure.

### Lindy — the strongest proof and risk reversal

![Lindy](shots/lindy.jpg)

"Hire Lindy, get it done." A face. A number ("set up in 2 minutes"). A testimonial line that is
just a row of logos plus a rating, framed as "Check my references" — the hire metaphor carried all
the way into the proof. $50 in credits is a risk reversal a buyer can price.

### Notion AI — the same promise, from the incumbent

![Notion AI](shots/notion-ai.jpg)

"Meet your 24/7 AI team." Autark's differentiation has to survive a buyer who already pays for
Notion: own PC, own model plans, agents that run the machine, not a page.

### Devin and OpenHands — one excellent teammate vs an open platform

![Devin](shots/devin.jpg)
![OpenHands](shots/openhands.jpg)

Devin sells a colleague ("your team's autonomous software engineer", "your teammate's favourite
teammate"). OpenHands sells the platform and the exit ("your agents, your models, your runtime").
Autark is closer to OpenHands in structure and closer to Devin in promise; the page does not yet
pick.

- **CrewAI** is enterprise-facing and reads as plumbing: "build & runtime", not "what you get".
- **Letta** leads with the research lab and memory as a thesis. Strong for developers, cold for a
  buyer who wants their week back.
- **Dyad** does the local/open promise with a working builder in the corner: "real code you own".
- **Open WebUI / LibreChat** win on freedom and lose on outcome; both are interfaces, not teams.
- **n8n** sells understanding: "see + understand + trust your agents" - the control argument, made
  visually.
- **Claude / Codex** are capability pages with real product surfaces, and they set the bar for
  motion and finish without ever saying "AI team".

## What Autark already wins

1. **A category argument, not a feature list.** "It still waits" is the only line in the set that
   names the problem the whole category has.
2. **Ownership as the product.** No account, no subscription from us, data on your PC - a trust
   position none of the hosted competitors can copy.
3. **Typography and restraint.** The wordmark-led hero is the most confident design in the set.
4. **A real app to show.** Most competitors show a marketing render; Autark has a window.

## Where the leaders are ahead

| Gap | Who shows it | What it costs Autark |
|---|---|---|
| The product above the fold | Manus, Dyad, Codex | a visitor cannot see the team working in the first five seconds |
| Named people, not roles | Lindy (a face), Devin (a name) | agents stay abstract; nobody feels they hired anyone |
| A number or a receipt | Lindy (2 minutes), n8n (workflow you can read) | the promise is believable but not yet evidenced |
| Price in the open | Lindy, Dyad, Codex | "free download" answers the wrong question: what does the work cost me |
| States and failure shown | nobody | the first real error is the buyer's first surprise |
| Motion that demonstrates | Manus, Codex | the page describes autonomy; it does not show it happening |

## The gap list, as moves

Fifteen moves with effort and expected effect are in [`../../UPGRADES.md`](../../autark-os/UPGRADES.md),
rows 1-15. The three that matter most:

1. **A first screen that runs.** Put one real artifact the team produced (a brief, a price table, a
   shipped page) with the agent that made it, at the top. Effort: medium. Effect: highest.
2. **Name the team.** Three agents with names, faces and one line each: what they own. Effort: low.
   Effect: high - it converts "a company" from a claim into a mental picture.
3. **Show the brake, not just the gas.** One screen of Secure mode: what is closed, what asks, what
   never happens. Effort: low (it exists). Effect: high - it is the trust differentiator, unshown.
